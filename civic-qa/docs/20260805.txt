LEGISLATION

A RESOLUTION
BY MRS. TURBEN                           NO 1953

A RESOLUTION TO AUTHORIZE AND DIRECT THE DIRECTOR OF FINANCE TO CERTIFY CERTAIN DELINQUENT CHARGES FOR CUTTING NOXIOUS WEEDS AND NUISANCE PLANT MATERIAL TO THE COUNTY AUDITOR FOR COLLECTION.

A copy of the resolution, having been placed on the desks of each member of Council prior to introduction, was read by title.

Mrs. Turben explained that each year, the City incurs costs for mowing and clearing overgrown yards and removing nuisance plant material when owners do not take care of their property.  The City bills property owners for those costs and most of them pay.  For those who do not pay, this is the annual resolution to certify the costs to the County Auditor so they can be added to the tax bill for each property.  This certification includes 11 properties at a total outstanding dollar amount of $3,753.51.

Thereupon, it was moved by Mrs. Turben and seconded by Vice Mayor Byington that the resolution be passed.

AN ORDINANCE
BY VICE MAYOR BYINGTON      NO 5040

AN ORDINANCE TO ENACT NEW SECTION 145.10, CONTRACT PROVISIONS, OF THE OAKWOOD ADMINISTRATIVE CODE, TO PROHIBIT THE INCLUSION OF CERTAIN TERMS IN THE CITY’S PUBLIC CONTRACTS.

A copy of the ordinance, having been placed on the desks of each member of Council prior to introduction, was read by title.

Vice Mayor Byington explained that this is first reading of an addition to the City’s purchasing ordinance.  Ohio law prohibits municipalities from signing certain kinds of contract provisions that are otherwise common in the private sector.  For example, cities are not allowed to agree to indemnify another party, and they are not allowed to sign a contract that imposes a confidentiality requirement that violates the Ohio Public Records Act.

Most contractors understand these legal requirements and take these provisions out when they are dealing with a public entity.  But sometimes, contractors who work on a national or even global scale do not understand legal requirements that are specific to Ohio, and they may be unwilling to amend their form documents.

The Law Director has recommended that the purchasing ordinance be updated to prohibit these unlawful contract terms, ensuring that the City is not bound by provisions that a contractor may refuse to delete.

This was the first reading of the ordinance, so Vice Mayor Byington made no motion.

STAFF REPORT

Human Resources Director Sangita Anand presented an overview of the Human Resources Department’s 2026 progress, current initiatives, and future priorities.

Mrs. Anand reviewed the department’s mission to support City operations through strategic workforce planning, recruitment and retention, employee development, wellness and benefits administration, labor relations, and organizational support. She highlighted improvements to the City’s recruitment and onboarding processes, including updated job descriptions, cross-department interview panels, structured candidate evaluations, and enhanced employee orientation.

The presentation also outlined the department’s ongoing commitment to employee development through leadership training, workplace safety education, regulatory compliance training, and professional development opportunities. Mrs. Anand discussed wellness initiatives and the City’s continued efforts to evaluate healthcare options and implement cost-saving strategies while maintaining competitive employee benefits.

Mrs. Anand reported that in 2026 the department updated 11 job descriptions, filled 20 vacancies with four additional recruitments in progress, supported three employee promotions, coordinated six employee training programs, implemented seven wellness initiatives, managed four retirements and employee departures, and continued labor negotiations and compliance support.

Looking ahead, the Human Resources Department will continue to focus on filling vacancies, strengthening recruitment and retention efforts, expanding employee development opportunities, supporting labor relations, advancing succession planning, and identifying long-term healthcare cost-saving opportunities. Ms. Anand also outlined the department’s 2027 goals, which include updating the City’s personnel regulations, expanding leadership development, continuing to evaluate employee health insurance options, and enhancing workforce retention initiatives.

Mrs. Turben asked how many individuals are employed by the City of Oakwood. Mrs. Anand replied that the City employs approximately 90 full-time and part-time employees.

Mayor Duncan thanked Mrs. Anand for her presentation and her ongoing efforts in the Human Resources Department.

Mr. Dorf inquired about current challenges related to workforce retention. Mrs. Anand explained that factors such as employee retirements and evolving workforce expectations present ongoing challenges. She noted that many employees place greater emphasis on salary, benefits, work-life balance, and organizational culture, and are less likely to remain with one employer long-term. She added that the City continues to promote wellness programs and foster a welcoming environment.

Mr. Dorf thanked Ms. Anand for her efforts in helping to build a culture of care within the organization.
